Bundling Home and Auto Insurance: Real Savings or Myth?

Marcus Reyes Insurance & Risk Writer Updated Aug 6, 2026 7 min read

"Bundle and save" is one of the most repeated phrases in insurance advertising, and like most repeated marketing phrases, it's true often enough to stick around but not true universally enough to trust blindly. Bundling home and auto insurance with the same company genuinely does save many people real money — but "many people" isn't "everyone," and the only way to know which category you're in is to actually run the numbers rather than assume.

Why bundling discounts exist in the first place

Insurers offer multi-policy discounts for a few straightforward business reasons: acquiring and retaining a customer across two policies is more valuable to them than one, bundled customers tend to switch insurers less often (which reduces the insurer's marketing costs), and in some cases, insurers view customers who bundle as a slightly lower-risk pool overall. The discount is a real, calculated incentive — typically somewhere in the range of 5–25% off one or both policies, depending on the insurer and your specific situation — not just a marketing gimmick.

Where bundling can quietly cost you money instead

The catch is that "20% off" only matters relative to what the starting price actually is. An insurer can offer a generous-sounding bundling discount while still pricing both policies higher overall than a competitor's unbundled rates. Bundling discounts are a discount off that specific insurer's price — not a guarantee that insurer has the lowest price to begin with.

A common trap: Insurer A might offer 20% off when you bundle home and auto, landing at a combined price of $2,800/year. Insurer B, with no bundling discount at all, might quote $2,500/year total for the same two policies purchased separately from two different companies. The "bigger discount percentage" doesn't automatically mean the lower total cost.

How to actually check whether bundling saves you money

  1. Get a bundled quote from at least one or two insurers that offer both home and auto coverage.
  2. Get separate, unbundled quotes from other insurers for auto only and home only — including insurers that specialize in just one type of coverage, since some of the most competitive home-only or auto-only insurers don't even offer bundling.
  3. Add up the separate quotes' total and compare directly against the bundled total, using identical coverage levels and deductibles across every quote so you're comparing like for like.
  4. Factor in service quality, not just price — if you've had a good claims experience with a particular insurer, that's worth something too, even if a competitor is marginally cheaper.

Beyond the discount: other reasons bundling can be worth it

  • Simplicity — one bill, one login, one point of contact for questions, which has genuine value even when it isn't the absolute cheapest option.
  • Claims coordination — in situations where a single event affects both your home and vehicle (a major storm, for example), having one insurer handling both claims can streamline the process.
  • Loyalty-based perks — some insurers offer additional benefits to bundled, long-tenured customers, like accident forgiveness or reduced deductibles, on top of the base discount.

When unbundling makes more sense

  • You live in a region where one insurer is clearly the strongest specialist for home insurance (say, due to local disaster risk expertise) but isn't competitively priced for auto, or vice versa.
  • You have a driving record or vehicle type that makes you a higher-risk auto customer, which can sometimes push your combined bundled price higher than shopping the auto policy separately with an insurer that specializes in higher-risk drivers.
  • You've done the actual comparison and the unbundled total is meaningfully lower — in which case, the discount percentage on the bundle is beside the point.

Standard risk profile, want simplicityBundling often works well and is worth the convenience

High-value or unusual home (older home, high-risk area)Worth comparing a specialty home insurer separately
Haven't shopped rates in 3+ yearsRe-quote both bundled and unbundled — pricing shifts more than people expect
Bundling is a genuinely useful discount mechanism, not a guaranteed best price. Treat the bundled quote as one option to compare against the market, not as the end of the shopping process.

A practical approach

Start by getting a bundled quote from your current insurer or a couple of major carriers, then spend the extra twenty minutes getting a separate auto quote and a separate home quote from at least one other source each. If the bundled total wins, take the discount and enjoy the simplicity. If it doesn't, there's no rule saying you have to use the same company for both — plenty of people successfully run separate home and auto policies with two different insurers and come out ahead.
